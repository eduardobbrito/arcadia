Arcadia

An Arc-style sidebar, spaces and a command bar for Chrome.

Set it up

  1. Open the sidebar. Click the Arcadia icon in Chrome's toolbar, or press . Pin the icon from the puzzle-piece menu so it stays visible.
  2. Move it to the left. In Chrome's Appearance settings, set “Side panel position” to left.
  3. Keep one sidebar. If Chrome's vertical tabs are on, you will see two. Right-click Chrome's tab strip and choose “Show Tabs Horizontally”. Chrome does not let an extension restyle or hide that strip, so this step is yours.
  4. Press . The command bar opens over the page you are on. Type an address, a search, or the name of an open tab.
  5. Add a space. Use the + at the bottom of the sidebar, then swipe sideways with two fingers on the sidebar to move between spaces. Drag a tab above the line to pin it to the space.
  6. Want separate logins? Chrome only separates cookies between Chrome profiles. Add Arcadia to each profile; each one gets its own sidebar and spaces.

Chrome's tab strip along the top cannot be removed, but in full screen it hides if you turn off View › Always Show Toolbar in Full Screen. Then switch on “Back, forward and address in the sidebar” below.

Shortcuts

Chrome assigns a shortcut only when the keys are free, and never lets an extension take itself. Change any of these on Chrome's shortcuts page.

Privacy

Arcadia makes no network requests. Its pages are locked by a content-security policy that blocks every outside connection, so it cannot send anything anywhere.

It keeps no record of what you browse: no visited pages, no clicks, no history of its own. The only things saved are what you create here, in this Chrome profile: your spaces, the tabs you pin and these settings.
