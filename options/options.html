Arcadia

An Arc-style sidebar, spaces with their own logins, and a command bar for Firefox.

Set it up

  1. Open the sidebar. Click the Arcadia icon in Firefox's toolbar, or press .
  2. Hide Firefox's own tab strip. An extension is not allowed to do this, so it takes a small file in your Firefox profile: firefox/userChrome.css from Arcadia's folder. The README has the three steps.
  3. Press . The command bar opens over the page you are on. Type an address, a search, or the name of an open tab.
  4. Add a space. Use the + at the bottom of the sidebar. Each new space is a Firefox container, so it has its own logins: sign in to one account in Work and another in Personal.
  5. Move between spaces. Swipe sideways with two fingers on the sidebar, or use the icons at the bottom. Tabs from other spaces are hidden, including from Ctrl+Tab.

Firefox may ask whether Arcadia may keep tabs hidden. Choose “Keep Tabs Hidden”; that is how other spaces stay out of the way.

Shortcuts

Firefox keeps for itself, so Arcadia swaps the new tab for the command bar instead of taking the key.

Open the Add-ons Manager, click the gear at the top and choose “Manage Extension Shortcuts”.

Privacy

Arcadia makes no network requests. Its pages are locked by a content-security policy that blocks every outside connection, so it cannot send anything anywhere.

It keeps no record of what you browse: no visited pages, no clicks, no history of its own. The only things saved are what you create here, in this Firefox profile: your spaces, the tabs you pin (with their site icon) and these settings.
